    \begin{abstract}
>>> We construct a symmetric thin-shell wormhole from two copies of the positive-lapse exterior of a rationally regularized Letelier--Alencar string-cloud black hole with a finite-curvature anti--de Sitter core and string-cloud asymptotics. Using the Darmois--Israel formalism, we derive the surface stresses, energy-condition combinations, conservation law, and effective potential. Every admissible static throat has negative surface energy density, so the weak and dominant energy conditions are violated, whereas the null condition restricted to directions tangent to the shell, and consequently the intrinsic strong condition, holds only in the inner sampled exterior. The radial-vacuum relation of the bulk source removes the external momentum flux. We derive a general local-stability criterion for density- and radius-dependent equations of state and apply it to five surface-fluid models, calibrating one parameter at each static throat. Across the sampled families, barotropic shells remain unstable, whereas the other closures can admit parameter-dependent stable branches. An exact unregularized control shows that the energy-condition structure and the radial-stability signs of the first three closures persist without the rational regulator, whereas the cosmic-Chaplygin stability sign can change while the selected calibration branch remains real and simple. Backward ray tracing shows that throat location primarily reorganizes higher-order and through-throat images, while string-cloud strength and inclination control annular structure and Doppler asymmetry. Comparisons of one- and two-sided source configurations isolate genuine far-side images and show that the central dark region depends on the source distribution.
    \end{abstract}

    Traversable wormholes provide a particularly sharp arena for testing how spacetime topology, causal structure, and the material content of gravitation constrain one another. In the Morris--Thorne framework, a throat connecting two regions can remain horizon free and traversable only if its geometry satisfies a flare-out condition whose realization in general relativity is tied to violations of the classical energy conditions \cite{MorrisThorne1988,MorrisThorneYurtsever1988,HochbergVisser1998}. This local requirement is reinforced by global results such as topological censorship, which excludes observable nontrivial topology under appropriate causal, asymptotic, and averaged-null-energy assumptions \cite{FriedmanSchleichWitt1993}. Quantum field theory does permit negative-energy densities in restricted circumstances, but quantum inequalities severely constrain their magnitude, duration, and spatial distribution \cite{FordRoman1996}. Consequently, proposals that reduce the integrated amount of exotic matter must still be distinguished from constructions that genuinely evade local or quantum restrictions \cite{VisserKarDadhich2003,FewsterRoman2005}. These considerations do not remove wormholes from theoretical study; rather, they make the localization, interpretation, and dynamical response of the supporting source central questions.
    
>>> Thin-shell wormholes address the localization problem by replacing a smooth exotic-matter distribution with a distributional source confined to a timelike junction. The mathematical basis is the junction formalism for non-null hypersurfaces, in which the jump of the extrinsic curvature determines the surface stress tensor \cite{Israel1966}. Visser's surgical construction showed that two exterior regions can be glued at a spherical boundary to produce a traversable geometry whose nontrivial matter content is concentrated on the throat \cite{Visser1989}. Poisson and Visser subsequently cast radial perturbations of a static shell as motion in an effective potential and obtained the standard linearized stability criterion \cite{PoissonVisser1995}. This framework has since been generalized to arbitrary spherically symmetric seed metrics, asymmetric junctions, momentum fluxes, charged backgrounds, cosmological terms, higher dimensions, and different throat topologies \cite{Eiroa2008SphericalStability,GarciaLoboVisser2012,EiroaRomero2004Charged,LoboCrawford2004,DiasLemos2010}. The geometric interpretation of flare-out also requires care for a distributional throat, because conditions derived for smooth wormhole geometries cannot always be transferred unchanged to a thin shell \cite{MazharimousaviHalilsoy2014FlareOut}. Reviews of the subject emphasize that the seed geometry and the shell equation of state are equally important: changing either one can alter the admissible static domain and the sign of the second derivative of the effective potential \cite{KokubuHarada2020,RadhakrishnanEtAl2024Review}.
    
    Regular black holes supply an especially useful class of seed spacetimes because they replace the central curvature singularity by a finite-curvature core while retaining horizon structures that can be compared with their singular counterparts. Modern exact examples arise from nonlinear electrodynamics, effective matter distributions, limiting-curvature constructions, and running-coupling improvements \cite{AyonBeatoGarcia1998,AyonBeatoGarcia2005,Bronnikov2001RegularMagnetic,Dymnikova2004RegularCharged,Hayward2006,Frolov2016,IshibashiOhtaYamaguchi2021}. Their sources commonly satisfy the radial-vacuum relation $T^t{}_t=T^r{}_r$, although the remaining energy conditions depend on the detailed anisotropy and regularization mechanism \cite{Zaslavskii2010,BalartVagenas2014}. Regularity by itself therefore does not determine the properties of a thin-shell wormhole constructed from the exterior: the junction excises the core, while the lapse function inherited from the regular geometry controls the surface stresses and radial dynamics. This separation has motivated thin-shell studies based on Hayward and other regular geometries, black-bounce spacetimes, and quantum-corrected backgrounds \cite{HalilsoyOvgunMazharimousavi2014,SharifMumtaz2016Hayward,LoboSimpsonVisser2020,JavedEtAl2023QuantumCorrected,Javed2024}. Related work in massive, scalar--tensor, and nonminimally coupled theories further shows that conclusions about exoticity are theory dependent; surface matter satisfying standard energy conditions can occur when the junction equations differ from those of ordinary Einstein gravity \cite{TangphatiEtAl2020dRGT,ForghaniEtAl2020EnergyConditions,EiroaEtAl2026ScalarTensor}.

    A distinct line of development concerns black holes sourced by clouds or fluids of strings. Letelier formulated the covariant stress tensor of a continuous distribution of one-dimensional strings and obtained a Schwarzschild-like exterior modified by a constant string contribution \cite{Letelier1979}. The model has since been extended to more general string fluids with nonconstant constitutive behavior and has been used to study horizons, thermodynamics, geodesics, and shadows \cite{Santos2025StringFluids}. Alencar, Landim, and Costa Filho introduced an additional magnetic-like component in the string bivector, producing a hypergeometric correction to the lapse while recovering the Letelier regime in the appropriate limit \cite{Alencar2025}. The resulting geometry remains singular at the center. Muniz \textit{et al.} then applied a rational Dagum-type regulator to this Letelier--Alencar source and obtained an exact black-hole solution with finite curvature invariants, a locally anti--de Sitter core, and a string-cloud exterior \cite{Muniz2026}. That work mapped the horizon sector and examined energy conditions, thermodynamics, and shadow observables, thereby providing both the direct seed metric and the physical parameter domain used in the present construction. The appearance of an anti--de Sitter core also places this solution within the wider family of nonsingular geometries whose central vacuum structure differs from the more common de Sitter core.

    String matter has already appeared in several thin-shell settings, but the existing constructions address different seed geometries. Richarte and Simeone built thin-shell wormholes directly from the singular Letelier string-cloud spacetime and analyzed the amount of exotic matter, traversability, and radial stability \cite{RicharteSimeone2008}. The modified cosmic form used below is taken as a surface closure only; its functional ancestry in interacting cosmological fluids does not imply that viscosity, scale-factor dependence, or a volumetric cosmological interpretation is inherited by the shell \cite{SadeghiFarahani2013}. The polymer-black-hole analysis of Javed \textit{et al.} is a particularly close methodological comparison because it treats barotropic, variable phantom, and variable Chaplygin laws within the same symmetric thin-shell problem \cite{Javed2024}.
    
    Beyond the standard requirement that the effective potential have positive curvature at equilibrium, recent studies have clarified complementary aspects of thin-shell stability. Thermodynamic stability is logically distinct from radial mechanical stability, although both can be imposed on the same equilibrium family \cite{ForghaniEtAl2019Thermodynamic,EiroaEtAl2024ChargedThermo}. For barotropic shells with vanishing momentum flux, the radial-stability criterion can be related to photon and antiphoton spheres of the seed spacetime \cite{TsukamotoKokubu2024PhotonOrbits}. Conversely, under the assumptions of an asymptotically flat seed and a barotropic surface fluid, an equilibrium throat placed at the innermost photon sphere is generically unstable \cite{Mazharimousavi2024InnermostPhotonSphere}. These results underscore the need to state the asymptotic structure, flux term, and equation-of-state assumptions explicitly before transferring a stability conclusion from one background to another.

>>> Against this dynamical background, the optical appearance provides a complementary probe of how the same junction reorganizes null geodesics. Although wormholes remain hypothetical, horizon-scale very-long-baseline interferometry at $1.3\,\mathrm{mm}$ has reconstructed the compact, gravitationally lensed emission surrounding M87* and Sgr~A*, making the near-critical image morphology of supermassive compact objects an important observational probe of strong gravitational fields \cite{theeventhorizontelescopecollaborationFirstM87Event2019,collaborationFirstSagittariusEvent2022}. These measurements are not direct optical images of event horizons; rather, they resolve synchrotron emission on horizon scales. Theoretically, such images have been studied mainly through two complementary approaches: general-relativistic ray tracing of radiation propagation and analyses of features such as the shadow, lensing ring, and photon ring as probes of strong-field spacetime geometry \cite{luminetImageSphericalBlack1979a,vincentGYOTONewGeneral2011,grallaBlackHoleShadows2019,grallaShapeBlackHole2020}. Previous studies have shown that image morphology depends not only on the propagation geometry but also on the emission model, electrodynamic corrections, magnetic fields, accretion-flow structure, and boundary or junction conditions \cite{huQEDEffectBlack2021a,zengQEDAccretionFlow2022,houImageKerrMelvinBlack2022,zhangImagingThickAccretion2024a,caoImagingSignaturesIsrael2026,wangHowDifferentAre2023}. Consequently, the observed shadow and ring structures generally arise from the combined effects of spacetime geometry and the emitting environment. Although a wormhole can locally possess an exterior geometry closely resembling that of a black hole, it changes the global continuation of null geodesics and may therefore produce distinct imaging signatures. Previous studies have found that the wormhole topology and the symmetry between its two exterior regions can affect the shadow boundary and ring structure and may introduce additional image components. For reflection-symmetric or two-ended wormholes, rays traversing the throat can carry radiation from emitters in the other exterior to the observer's side, producing additional images or light rings while preserving an overall black-hole-like appearance \cite{ohgamiWormholeShadows2015,paulObservationalSignaturesWormholes2020,olmoNewLightRings2022,ruedaTraversableWormholesMultiple2025}. For reflection-asymmetric thin-shell wormholes, the inequivalent photon effective potentials on the two sides can allow the far-side photon sphere to contribute to the shadow edge and can generate double shadows, additional photon rings, or lensing bands \cite{wielgusReflectionasymmetricWormholesTheir2020,wangNovelShadowsAsymmetric2020,pengObservationalSignatureAdditional2021a,guerreroDoubleShadowsReflectionasymmetric2021,Tsukamoto2021DoubleShadows,heObservationalAppearanceExtra2024}. This optical context complements the energy-condition and stability questions above and leads to the combined problem addressed here.

>>> This paper is organized as follows. Section~\ref{sec:seed} reviews the regularized Letelier--Alencar black-hole geometry, its core and asymptotic limits, and the admissible positive-lapse exterior. Section~\ref{sec:construction} constructs the symmetric junction and derives the induced geometry, unit normals, and extrinsic curvature. Section~\ref{sec:surface} obtains the surface stresses and analyzes the energy conditions. Thin-disk images show an additional strong inclination dependence: an almost face-on system is dominated by concentric annuli, whereas a nearly edge-on disk develops a narrow direct band, strong Doppler asymmetry, and well-separated lensed arcs. Greater image multiplicity does not imply uniform brightening under the common intensity normalization. When identical disks are placed in both universes, through-throat radiation produces distinct central and inner counterimages that are absent for a one-sided disk, thereby separating genuine far-side emission from same-side higher-order images. A dark central region in the one-sided model therefore depends on the assumed source distribution and disk truncation, as well as on the null-geodesic structure, and should not automatically be identified with a black-hole capture shadow.
